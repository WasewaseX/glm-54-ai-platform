Chapter 25 — The Patent Plot

The institute's entrance hall buzzes with tension as Commander Rostova strides in, her uniform crisp and her expression unreadable. Marcus Thorne greets her with forced enthusiasm while Elara watches from the shadows, her heart pounding. The commander moves with a military precision that seems out of place in the academic environment, her polished boots clicking against the polished marble floor. Behind her, four security specialists fan out, their eyes scanning every corner of the space as if searching for threats that only they could perceive.

"Commander Rostova, welcome to the Oceanic Research Institute," Marcus says, extending a hand that she barely acknowledges with a brief, firm shake. "We're honored by your visit. I trust your journey was pleasant?"

"Efficient, Doctor Thorne," she replies, her voice a low contralto that carries without effort. "The OmniMarine jet is always on time. I appreciate your institute's cooperation in this matter."

"Of course," Marcus says, gesturing toward the seating area. "Would you care for refreshments before we begin the inspection?"

"No, thank you," Rostova says, already moving toward the main security console near the entrance. "I'd prefer to get started immediately. My team needs to assess all communication systems first."

Elara watches from behind a marble column, her breath catching in her throat. Something about Rostova's demeanor sets her teeth on edge. The commander isn't here for a routine inspection; she's here on a mission. Elara's fingers twitch at her sides, the urge to retreat to her lab and barricade herself inside overwhelming her rational mind. She forces herself to stay, knowing that knowledge is power, even when that knowledge terrifies her.

Elara slips away from the entrance hall, her footsteps silent on the marble floor. She makes her way to the cybersecurity wing where Lena's office is located. The corridor is unusually quiet, most researchers having been advised to avoid the main areas during Rostova's inspection. Elara quickens her pace, her mind racing with possibilities. What could OmniMarine possibly want with the bioreactor beyond their already substantial funding?

Lena's door is slightly ajar. Elara pushes it open to find the cybersecurity specialist hunched over her monitors, her face illuminated by the glow of multiple screens.

"Lena," Elara whispers, stepping into the office.

Lena startles, then relaxes when she sees Elara. "I thought I heard someone. What's happening out there? Rostova's got everyone on edge."

"She's not here for a routine inspection," Elara says, closing the door behind her. "There's something deliberate about her movements. She's already taken control of the security systems."

Lena nods grimly. "I noticed. Her team has been rerouting all external communications through their own servers. They're monitoring everything—email, data transfers, even the internal messaging system."

"Why?" Elara asks, though she suspects she already knows the answer.

"I don't know yet," Lena says, turning back to her screens. "But I've been working around their monitoring. There's something else. I've detected unusual activity in the bioreactor's data streams. It looks like someone's been accessing the dreaming logs remotely."

Elara's blood runs cold. "OmniMarine?"

"Probably," Lena confirms. "The access patterns are consistent with their corporate servers. But they're not just observing—they're downloading the data. Large chunks of it."

Elara feels a familiar mix of anger and protectiveness wash over her. The bioreactor's dreams—whatever they might be—were hers first. She'd been the one to document them, to analyze their strange patterns, to feel the uncanny resemblance to some form of consciousness forming in the machine.

"We have to find out what they're doing with that data," Elara says, moving closer to Lena's desk.

"I'm trying," Lena says, her fingers dancing across the keyboard. "But they're good. Really good. They've encrypted their own communications and masked their tracks. It's like they're ghosts in our system."

Elara watches as Lena works, her mind racing. "Can you trace where the data is being sent?"

"Not directly," Lena admits. "But I might be able to intercept some of their encrypted files if I can find a vulnerability in their routing protocol."

Elara nods, her determination hardening. "Do it. Whatever it takes."

Lena turns back to her screens, her expression focused. "This might take some time. I'll need to bypass several firewalls."

"I'll wait," Elara says, pulling up a chair beside Lena's desk. The two women sit in silence as Lena works, the only sound the soft clicking of keys and the hum of the computers.

After what feels like an eternity, Lena straightens up in her chair. "I think I've got something."

"What is it?" Elara asks, leaning closer to the screen.

"I've managed to intercept a small portion of their encrypted data transfer," Lena explains. "It's heavily protected, but I've been able to decrypt a few fragments."

On the screen, lines of code and text fragments flash by too quickly for Elara to process.

"Slow down," she says.

Lena pauses the scrolling, pointing to a section of text. "Look at this."

Elara squints at the screen, trying to make sense of the technical jargon mixed with what appears to be corporate planning documents.

"What am I looking at?"

"These fragments are from a larger document," Lena says. "It seems to be outlining some kind of strategic initiative for the bioreactor technology."

Elara's stomach tightens. "OmniMarine's plans?"

"Looks that way," Lena confirms. "There's mention of 'proprietary technology' and 'commercial viability.' But it's all encrypted fragments. I need more to understand the full picture."

"We need to get into their systems," Elara says, a plan forming in her mind. "There must be a way to access their files directly."

Lena shakes her head. "Their security is top-notch. Breaking in would take weeks, maybe months, and they'd detect the attempt immediately."

"We don't have weeks," Elara says, thinking of the bioreactor's dreams being carted off to some corporate lab to be dissected and claimed as their own. "There has to be another way."

Lena's fingers pause over the keyboard. "There might be one other option. If we can find someone on the inside—someone with access to their systems who might be willing to help."

"Who?" Elara asks, though she already suspects the answer.

"Aris," Lena says quietly. "He has access to the bioreactor's core programming. If he's working with OmniMarine—and from what you've told me, he is—he might be able to get us into their systems without raising suspicion."

Elara hesitates. Aris was brilliant, but he was also Marcus's son. Could he be trusted? Then again, if OmniMarine was planning to steal the technology from his father's institute, perhaps he'd be willing to help expose their plan.

"Can you contact him?" Elara asks.

Lena nods. "I can send him a secure message. But I'll need to phrase it carefully. If OmniMarine is monitoring all communications, we need to be discreet."

"Do it," Elara says. "Tell him we need to meet urgently. Something about the bioreactor's data integrity."

Lena nods, her fingers flying across the keyboard as she composes a message. "Sent. Now we wait."

Elara and Lena sit in tense silence, watching the door to Lena's office. Every passing minute feels like an eternity. Elara's mind races with possibilities—what would OmniMarine do with the bioreactor's technology if they got their hands on it completely? The thought sends a shiver down her spine.

After what feels like an hour, Lena's screen flashes with a new message.

"He replied," Lena says, opening the message. "He'll meet us in the biore observation lab in thirty minutes. Says he can slip away during Rostova's inspection."

"Good," Elara says, standing up. "Let's go."

The two women slip out of Lena's office and make their way through the deserted corridors. The institute feels like a different place now—no longer the sanctuary of scientific discovery, but a potential battleground for control over something that might be more valuable than anyone had imagined.

When they reach the biore observation lab, Aris is already there, pacing nervously. He looks up as they enter, his eyes wide with concern.

"What's going on?" he asks, his voice barely above a whisper. "Lena said it was urgent about the bioreactor's data integrity?"

"It's more than that," Elara says, closing the door behind them. "We need to know what OmniMarine is planning with the bioreactor's technology."

Aris's expression shifts from concern to alarm. "What do you mean? My father said they were just providing additional support for the project."

"Support?" Lena scoffs. "They're monitoring all communications, downloading the dreaming logs, and planning something big. We intercepted fragments of a document outlining some kind of strategic initiative."

Aris pales. "My father doesn't know about this. Does he?"

"We don't think so," Elara says. "But we need to find out what OmniMarine is really planning. We believe they intend to patent the dreaming technology as their own."

Aris runs a hand through his already disheveled hair. "That's impossible. The dreaming patterns are unique to the bioreactor's design. My father designed it himself."

"Or so he thinks," Lena says. "We need proof of their plans. We believe there's a document somewhere in their systems that outlines their intentions."

Aris hesitates, then nods. "I might be able to access their internal network. I have some backdoor access from when I was helping with their security protocols."

"Can you get us that document?" Elara asks.

Aris nods again. "I can try. But it'll take time, and I can't do it here. If Rostova's team is monitoring everything, they'll detect the intrusion."

"Where then?" Lena asks.

"The old server room," Aris says. "It's not connected to the main network anymore. My father keeps it as a backup system. It's the only place I can access OmniMarine's servers without being detected."

"Lead the way," Elara says.

The three of them slip out of the observation lab and make their way to the older part of the institute. The corridors here are dimmer, the air colder. Aris leads them through a maze of passages until they reach a heavy metal door marked "Server Room - Authorized Personnel Only."

Ais pulls out a keycard and swipes it. The door clicks open, revealing a room filled with humming servers and blinking lights. The air inside is thick with the smell of ozone and dust.

"Here we go," Aris says, moving to a terminal in the corner. He sits down and begins typing rapidly, his fingers flying across the keyboard.

Elara and Lena watch from a distance, their hearts pounding with anticipation. The only sound is the hum of the servers and Aris's fingers on the keyboard.

After several minutes, Aris straightens up. "I'm in. I've accessed their internal network. Now I need to find the document Lena mentioned."

Elara and Lena crowd closer to the screen as Aris begins searching through files. The text scrolling by is a mix of corporate jargon and technical specifications.

"Here it is," Aris says, stopping the scrolling. He opens a file labeled "OMNIMARINE STRATEGIC INITIATIVE."

The three of them lean closer to read the contents. Elara's breath catches in her throat as she scans the document.

"It's worse than we thought," Lena says quietly. "They're planning to patent the dreaming technology as a revolutionary AI breakthrough. They're calling it 'DreamCatcher AI'."

Elara continues reading, her growing horror evident on her face. The document outlines a plan to commercialize the technology without acknowledging the Oceanic Research Institute's contribution or sharing any profits. It describes the dreaming patterns as "proprietary technology to be developed by OmniMarine's R&D division."

On a decrypted document titled 'OMNIMARINE STRATEGIC INITIATIVE', Elara reads: 'The DreamCatcher AI represents a paradigm shift in machine consciousness—proprietary technology to be developed by OmniMarine's R&D division.'
