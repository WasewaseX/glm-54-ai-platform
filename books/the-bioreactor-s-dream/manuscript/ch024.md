Chapter 24 — Aris's Doubts

The institute's entrance hall buzzes with tension as Commander Rostova strides in, her uniform crisp and her expression unreadable. Marcus Thorne greets her with forced enthusiasm while Elara watches from the shadows, her heart pounding. The commander moves with a military precision that seems out of place in the academic environment, her polished boots clicking against the polished marble floor. Behind her, four security specialists fan out, their eyes scanning every corner of the space as if searching for threats that only they could perceive.

"Commander Rostova, welcome to the Oceanic Research Institute," Marcus says, extending a hand that she barely acknowledges with a brief, firm shake. "We're honored by your visit. I trust your journey was pleasant?"

"Efficient, Doctor Thorne," she replies, her voice a low contralto that carries without effort. "The OmniMarine jet is always on time. I appreciate your institute's cooperation in this matter."

"Of course," Marcus says, gesturing toward the seating area. "Would you care for refreshments before we begin the inspection?"

"No, thank you," Rostova says, already moving toward the main security console near the entrance. "I'd prefer to get started immediately. My team needs to assess all communication systems first."

Elara watches from behind a marble column, her breath catching in her throat. Something about Rostova's demeanor sets her teeth on edge. The commander isn't here for a routine inspection; she's here on a mission. Elara's fingers twitch at her sides, the urge to retreat to her lab and barricade herself inside overwhelming.

Aris Thorne stands near the far wall, observing the exchange with a different kind of tension. His father's performance makes him uncomfortable, the forced cheerfulness masking something Aris can't quite name. He notices how Rostova's team positions themselves strategically near the main corridors, their movements coordinated like a well-rehearsed dance.

"Dr. Thorne," Rostova says, turning her attention to him. "Your work on the bioreactor's interface protocols has been impressive. I've reviewed the preliminary reports."

"Thank you, Commander," Aris replies, surprised by the direct acknowledgment. "I'm still optimizing the response times."

"Efficiency is paramount in commercial applications," she says, her gaze unwavering. "We expect great things from this project."

Aris nods, but her words hang in the air. Commercial applications? The bioreactor was supposed to be a research tool, a stepping stone toward understanding deep-sea ecosystems. The corporate involvement had always been present, but Rostova's presence feels different—more invasive, more purposeful.

As the inspection begins, Elara slips away, her path clear for now. Rostova's team is too focused on the security systems to notice her departure. Aris watches his father lead the commander and her team toward the administrative wing, his curiosity piqued by their conversation.

"Dr. Thorne," one of Rostova's specialists calls out. "We need to install monitoring equipment on the bioreactor access terminals. Higher-level encryption protocols are being implemented."

Marcus nods eagerly. "Of course. Full cooperation. Aris will assist you with the technical specifications."

Aris's stomach tightens. His father hadn't mentioned anything about additional security measures or monitoring equipment. The bioreactor had been his domain, his project to oversee and develop. Now, suddenly, OmniMarine was inserting themselves directly into the system.

The next few days pass in a haze of increased security and restricted access. Elara finds her lab monitored, her communications logged. Lena, the cybersecurity specialist, grows increasingly frustrated as she discovers backdoors being installed in the institute's network.

"They're building a parallel system," Lena tells Elara one evening in the deserted cafeteria. "Everything we do is being funneled through a secure OmniMarine server. It's not just monitoring—they're mirroring our entire research infrastructure."

Elara's knuckles are white where she grips her coffee mug. "The dreaming patterns. If they're accessing those..."

"They're patenting it as proprietary AI technology," Lena confirms. "I've seen the draft filings. They're calling it 'Neuro-Quantum Adaptive Learning Architecture'—NQALA for short. No mention of the marine biology components, no reference to the bioreactor's actual function."

Aris overhears their conversation as he passes by the cafeteria entrance, pausing outside the doorway. His father had mentioned something about commercial applications, but this went beyond mere applications. They were stealing the core of the project, rebranding it as their own.

That night, Aris sits in his small office overlooking the dark ocean. The rhythmic crash of waves against the cliffs usually soothes him, but tonight it only amplifies his unease. He opens the bioreactor's core programming interface, his fingers flying across the keyboard. For months, he'd had unrestricted access to every aspect of the system, but now several sections are marked with red security warnings.

"ACCESS DENIED: LEVEL 5 CLEARANCE REQUIRED"

The designation is new. He tries his usual credentials, then his administrative override—both fail. Someone has locked him out of his own work.

Frustration gnaws at him. His father had always encouraged his involvement, praised his technical contributions. Why the sudden restrictions? And why hadn't Marcus mentioned these changes?

The next morning, Aris approaches his father in the crowded main lab. The space hums with activity, but Rostova's team is conspicuously absent today. Marcus is reviewing data on a holographic display, his brow furrowed in concentration.

"Father, I tried to access the core programming last night. Several sections are locked. Was I not informed about security changes?"

Marcus turns, his expression momentarily surprised before smoothing into practiced calm. "Ah, yes. Commander Rostova's team implemented new protocols yesterday. They're concerned about intellectual property protection. Some sections require higher clearance now."

"Intellectual property?" Aris repeats, the words tasting sour. "The bioreactor was developed here, with institute resources. OmniMarine provided funding, but this is our research."

Marcus sighs, gesturing for Aris to follow him to a quieter corner of the lab. "Let's not make a scene, Aris. The corporation has certain expectations about protecting their investment."

"What expectations? They're patenting our work as something else entirely."

Marcus's eyes narrow slightly. "Where are you getting these ideas?"

"Lena Petrova. She's seen the draft patents. They're calling it NQALA, completely omitting the marine biology components."

Marcus's jaw tightens. "That's standard corporate procedure. They're protecting their commercial interests. You should focus on your assigned tasks rather than getting distracted by administrative matters."

"But I can't access my own work now," Aris protests. "The interface protocols I developed are in those locked sections. How am I supposed to optimize them if I can't see them?"

Marcus places a hand on his shoulder, the gesture meant to be reassuring but feeling more like a warning. "Trust me, Aris. This is for the best. The project has grown beyond our initial scope. We need to trust the process."

Aris pulls away, his father's words ringing hollow. The trust he'd placed in Marcus, the admiration for his brilliance—it's fraying at the edges. Something is wrong here, and his father is complicit.

Later that afternoon, Aris sits at his terminal, determined to find answers. If his father won't be transparent, he'll dig for the truth himself. He begins by examining the project's funding history, cross-referencing research reports with OmniMarine's public statements about their biotechnology initiatives.

The discrepancies are subtle at first—minor inconsistencies in funding allocations that could be attributed to accounting errors. But as Aris digs deeper, patterns emerge. Research milestones that appear in internal reports are absent from public documentation. Data sets referenced in progress reports to the institute's board aren't mentioned in submissions to OmniMarine.

His fingers move across the keyboard with increasing urgency. He's not just a researcher; he's a programmer, a systems architect. He knows how to navigate complex digital landscapes, how to find what's hidden.

Late in the evening, the lab is nearly empty except for a few night-shift technicians. Aris isolates his terminal, running a deep scan of the project's shared server, searching for anomalies, for anything that might explain the sudden secrecy.

The scan reveals nothing at first—just the usual administrative files, research data, and project documentation. But then, something catches his eye: a hidden folder marked 'ARCHIVE', buried within layers of subdirectories that shouldn't exist in the project's organizational structure.

Curiosity overcoming caution, Aris clicks on the folder. It contains dozens of subfolders, each dated and cryptically labeled. He opens the most recent one, finding a series of encrypted documents. Using his administrative credentials, he bypasses the first layer of encryption, revealing a series of emails between his father and Commander Rostova.

Aris's breath catches as he begins reading the exchanges. The emails discuss 'proprietary technology' and 'commercial applications' with a cold detachment that chills him. There's no mention of the institute's research, no reference to marine biology or the bioreactor's actual purpose. The language is purely corporate, focused on patents, market positioning, and eliminating competition.

Aris's blood runs cold. His father had referred to Elara's research as "unauthorized," even though she was conducting it under the institute's auspices. And "appropriate measures"—what could that possibly mean? He scrolls through the thread, his heart pounding against his ribs. Another email from Rostova:

From: e.rostova@omnimarine.com
To: m.thorne@omnimarine.com

Understood. We've implemented additional monitoring on Dr. Vance's activities. Her access to the bioreactor has been restricted to Level 3 clearance. She cannot observe the dreaming cycles directly. If she persists in her inquiries, we'll need to consider alternative research assignments that would remove her from the project entirely.

Aris's stomach twists into a knot. They were isolating Elara, cutting her off from the very research that had consumed her. He remembered her passion, her obsession with the bioreactor's dreams, and now they were systematically dismantling her work. Why? Because it threatened their commercial narrative?

He continues reading, his fingers trembling slightly as he scrolls through the exchange. The emails reveal a coordinated effort to obscure the bioreactor's true nature, to present it as a technological breakthrough rather than a biological anomaly. They discussed scrubbing research papers, altering presentation materials, and redirecting funding to support the new narrative.

One email from Marcus dated three days ago references Aris himself:

From: m.thorne@omnimarine.com
To: e.rostova@omnimarine.com

Dr. Thorne has begun asking questions about security protocols. His technical knowledge makes him a potential liability. We need to ensure he remains focused on his assigned tasks without becoming aware of the full scope of our commercial strategy. Perhaps a more defined role within the project would help channel his abilities appropriately.

Aris feels a chill spread through his chest. His father saw him not as a collaborator but as a liability, someone to be managed and controlled. The admiration he'd always felt for Marcus, the belief that they shared a common passion for scientific discovery—it all felt like a lie now. Marcus was playing a different game, one where scientific integrity was sacrificed for corporate profit.

The final email in the thread is from Rostova, sent yesterday:

From: e.rostova@omnimarine.com
To: m.thorne@omnimarine.com

The NQALA demonstration is scheduled for next month. All preparations are on track. We've identified three potential competitors who might reverse-engineer our technology if they learn of its biological origins. Recommend we proceed with contingency plans to discredit any such attempts. Dr. Vance remains the primary concern. Her recent unauthorized attempts to access higher-level systems have been logged. If she persists, we may need to consider more permanent solutions.

Aris closes his eyes, the weight of those words pressing down on him. "More permanent solutions." What could that possibly mean in an academic research institute? Fear for Elara's safety wars with his own shock and betrayal. His father wasn't just compromising ethics; he was endangering people.

He saves the emails to a secure, encrypted drive, his movements automatic, his mind racing. What should he do? Confront his father? Go to Elara? Report this to the institute's board? Each option carries risks, and with Rostova's security team monitoring everything, he couldn't act without being detected.

The lab's overhead lights hum softly, casting long shadows across the empty workstations. Outside, the ocean continues its eternal rhythm, oblivious to the moral earthquake unfolding within these walls. Aris thinks of Elara, her fierce dedication to the bioreactor, her haunted eyes that spoke of both brilliance and pain. She deserved to know what was happening, deserved to understand why her work was being systematically dismantled.

He also thinks of his father, the man who had guided his scientific career, who had encouraged his passion for technology. Was there any chance Marcus was being coerced, that OmniMarine held leverage over him? Or had the pursuit of prestige and profit simply corrupted him beyond recognition?

Aris deletes his access logs for the 'ARCHIVE' folder, covering his digital tracks. He couldn't risk alerting Rostova's team to his discovery. But he also couldn't stay silent. The emails were clear—Elara was in danger, and the true nature of the bioreactor was being buried beneath corporate lies.

He closes the terminal, the screen going dark and reflecting his own troubled face. The man looking back at him was a stranger, someone who had believed in the purity of scientific inquiry, only to have that belief shattered by the cold reality of commercial exploitation. As he stands up, his resolve hardens. He wouldn't be a pawn in their game, wouldn't allow his father's ambition or OmniMarine's greed to destroy the bioreactor's potential or harm Elara.

He had to warn her, but not here. Not where they might be listening. He would find a way to speak with her privately, to show her what he'd discovered. Together, they might stand a chance against the corporate machine that threatened to consume them both. With that thought, Aris slips out of the lab and into the darkened corridor, his purpose clear and his steps silent against the marble floor.
