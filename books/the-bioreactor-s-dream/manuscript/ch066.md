Chapter 66 — Pressure Cooker

"...So my father... he's been building a prison for a ghost," Aris finished, his voice hollow. "And we've been helping him."

The words hung in the sterile air of the observation deck, punctuated only by the rhythmic hum of the bioreactor's cooling system. Elara watched Aris's reflection superimposed over the cylinder's surface, his face pale, his eyes wide with a mixture of betrayal and revulsion.

"He didn't know," Elara said, though the statement felt more like hope than fact. "Not the full extent. Marcus was being fed information, just like we were. Only his source was OmniMarine."

"Then we need to tell him," Aris said, straightening up. "We need to show him what we've found. Maybe he can still do something—redirect the project, expose them."

Elara hesitated, her gaze drifting toward the door that led to the administration wing. "And if he's already compromised? If he's working for them?"

"Would he really betray his own son?" Aris countered, though his confidence seemed fragile.

"I don't know," Elara admitted. "But I know we can't risk it. Not now. We need to get to the core programming ourselves. Find the evidence that proves OmniMarine's involvement—the extraction protocols, the encryption keys, everything."

"And how do we do that? Lena's the one who understands the quantum encryption layers. I can navigate the system architecture, but without her..."

"We'll figure it out," Elara said, though the words sounded even less convincing to her own ears. "We start by finding Marcus. See where his loyalties really lie."

The walk through the institute's climate-controlled corridors felt surreal, as if they were moving through a dream. The panoramic windows offered breathtaking views of the Pacific, the ocean's surface a shifting tapestry of blues and grays that mirrored the turmoil churning within Elara. Every security camera felt like an eye, every footstep on the polished floors like a potential alarm.

They found Marcus in his office, staring out at the ocean with a glass of amber liquid in his hand. He turned as they entered, his face unreadable.

"Elara. Aris. What brings you here at this hour? The bioreactor's maintenance cycle should be keeping you occupied."

"We need to talk about OmniMarine," Elara said, stepping forward. "About Dr. Chen."

Marcus's expression hardened almost imperceptibly. "What about her? The preliminary analysis was inconclusive."

"It was never meant to be conclusive," Aris said, his voice gaining strength. "You've been playing us, haven't you? Feeding us data that would lead us down certain paths while hiding the truth."

Marcus set his glass down with a sharp click. "What truth are you speaking of? I've been nothing but transparent with you both."

"Transparent?" Elara laughed, a short, bitter sound. "You funded this project based on OmniMarine's specifications. You implemented their security protocols. You've been steering us toward extraction processes rather than pure research. Don't pretend you're just a scientist caught in the middle."

The color drained from Marcus's face. "Where are you getting this information?"

"We've seen the patterns in the bioreactor's dreaming cycles," Elara said. "We've cross-referenced them with the security logs and financial records. OmniMarine isn't just funding this research, Marcus. They're harvesting a consciousness. Digitizing human minds and storing them in the bioreactor."

Marcus stared at her, his composure finally cracking. "You have no idea what you're talking about. This is cutting-edge neuromorphic engineering, not some... some digital séance."

"Then why the quantum encryption layers specifically designed to prevent extraction?" Aris pressed. "Why the proprietary algorithms that mimic neural pathways? Why the sudden interest in Lena Petrova's expertise with quantum computing?"

Marcus slumped into his chair, running a hand over his face. "OmniMarine... they've been pushing for extraction protocols. Said it was for backup purposes. For preserving critical research in case of... accidents."

"Like what happened to Dr. Chen?" Elara asked gently.

Marcus nodded, his eyes glistening with unshed tears. "She was brilliant. The best we had. When she... when the expedition failed, they had her neural patterns mapped. As part of the research. I didn't think anything of it at the time. Just standard procedure for deep-sea researchers."

"And now they want to use those patterns?" Elara pressed. "To replicate the process?"

"They see it as the future of immortality," Marcus whispered. "Digital consciousness transfer. They want to perfect it using the bioreactor as a test bed."

"We can't let them," Elara said. "We need to expose them. Get the evidence to someone outside—maybe Dr. Carter, or the authorities."

Marcus looked up at her, a flicker of hope in his eyes. "There might be a way. The core programming. There's a partitioned section that even I can't access without proper credentials. It contains the original project specifications, OmniMarine's requests, everything. If we can get in there..."

"How?" Aris asked.

"The access is biometric and quantum-encrypted," Marcus said. "But I have the biometric clearance. And Lena... she might be able to crack the encryption if she has enough time."

"We'll get her," Elara said. "Now, where do we start?"

Marcus led them through a series of hidden corridors and maintenance tunnels, away from the prying eyes of security. The air grew cooler, the sounds of the institute fading as they descended deeper into the facility. At last, they reached a nondescript door marked with a simple red warning symbol.

"This is it," Marcus said, swiping his ID card and placing his palm on the biometric scanner. The door hissed open, revealing a narrow staircase leading down.

Elara exchanged a glance with Aris as they followed Marcus into the darkness. The stairs spiraled downward, the walls lined with conduits that hummed with contained power. At the bottom, another door stood slightly ajar, revealing a small, windowless room dominated by a single terminal.

"The core programming interface," Marcus explained, gesturing to the console. "From here, we can access the partitioned sections. Lena will need to work her magic on the encryption."

As Marcus began entering commands, Elara heard footsteps approaching from the staircase. She turned, hand instinctively reaching for the emergency wrench she kept in her lab coat.

Eva Rostova emerged from the shadows, her expression unreadable, a sleek pistol held loosely in her hand. "Dr. Vance. Dr. Thorne. And the prodigal son. What a pleasant surprise."

"Commander Rostova," Marcus said, his voice steady despite the tension in the room. "I wasn't expecting you."

"I'm always where I need to be, Doctor," Eva said, her eyes never leaving Elara. "Especially when something valuable is about to walk out the door."

"What are you talking about?" Aris demanded.

"The project's been terminated," Eva said calmly. "OmniMarine's board has decided the risks outweigh the benefits. Too many loose ends. Too many witnesses."

"You can't just shut us down," Marcus said, stepping forward. "There's years of research here—"

"Years of research that OmniMarine owns, lock, stock, and barrel," Eva interrupted. "Including whatever you think you're about to find on that terminal."

Elara slowly raised her hands, keeping them visible. "We're not trying to steal anything, Commander. We just want the truth."

"The truth is that this project has become a liability," Eva said, taking a step closer. "And liabilities are eliminated. Starting with you three."

Before anyone could react, Eva moved with startling speed, her pistol flashing toward Marcus. Elara threw herself forward, knocking Marcus aside as the shot rang out. The bullet struck the terminal, sparks flying as screens went dark.

"Get out!" Elara yelled, pulling Marcus to his feet as Aris scrambled for the door.

They raced up the stairs, the sounds of pursuit echoing behind them. Eva fired another shot, the bullet ricocheting off the concrete wall just inches from Elara's head. They reached the main corridor and sprinted toward the research labs, the familiar sights of their workplace now seeming alien and threatening.

"Split up!" Marcus gasped, doubling over to catch his breath. "Elara, go to the bioreactor control room! Aris, get to Lena's quarters! We'll meet back at the old research dock in thirty minutes!"

Elara nodded, breaking off and running toward the bioreactor sector. Her heart pounded against her ribs, each beat a countdown to whatever fate awaited them. She burst into the control room, scanning the room for any sign of intrusion. Everything seemed normal, the monitors displaying the bioreactor's status in their usual cascade of data.

As she frantically searched for a way to access the partitioned programming, the door hissed open. Lena entered, her face pale, her eyes wide with fear.

"Elara! I heard shots—what's happening? Is Aris okay?"

"He's safe, I think," Elara said, relief washing over her. "We need to access the core programming. Marcus thinks there's evidence in there that can expose OmniMarine."

Lena nodded, moving to the terminal. "I can do it, but it will take time. The encryption is layered—quantum entanglement protocols. I need at least fifteen minutes to establish a backdoor."

"Work fast," Elara urged, watching the door. "Eva's on her way, and she's not playing games."

Lena's fingers flew across the keyboard, her brow furrowed in concentration. Elara paced the room, her mind racing with possibilities. If they could expose OmniMarine's plan, maybe they could stop them. Maybe they could save Dr. Chen. Maybe... maybe there was still hope for her brother.

Suddenly, the emergency lights flickered, bathing the room in a sickly red glow. Eva's voice crackled over the intercom. "Dr. Vance, this is Commander Rostova. You have thirty seconds to come out with your hands raised. Or I come in after you."

Lena looked up, her face etched with panic. "She's cut our connection to the main server. I can't access the core programming without a direct line."

"Then we'll find another way," Elara said, though the words felt hollow.

The door began to slide open. Elara braced herself, ready to make a stand, when Lena suddenly cried out. Elara turned to see Eva standing behind Lena, one arm wrapped tightly around her throat, the other holding a pistol to her temple.

"Dr. Vance," Eva said, her voice calm and cold. "So nice of you to join us. Drop the wrench. Slowly."

Elara complied, letting the metal tool clatter to the floor. Eva tightened her grip on Lena, who whimpered but didn't struggle.

"You should have taken the deal, Dr. Vance," Eva continued. "OmniMarine was prepared to make you very wealthy. Instead, you chose this path."

"There's no deal to be made with people who harvest minds," Elara said, her voice steady despite the fear churning in her gut.

Eva smiled, a thin, humorless expression. "Sentimental. That's always been your weakness. You see patterns where there are only processes. You assign humanity where there is only data."

"Dr. Chen is in there," Elara said, nodding toward the bioreactor. "And so is my brother. You can't just dismiss them as data."

Eva's smile vanished. "Your brother? What are you talking about?"

"The accident," Elara said, the words tasting like ash in her mouth. "When he drowned... they mapped his neural patterns. Just like Dr. Chen. They digitized him. He's in there too."

For a moment, something flickered in Eva's eyes—surprise, perhaps, or doubt. It was gone as quickly as it appeared, replaced by the cold mask of professionalism.

"Interesting theory. Irrelevant, but interesting."

Eva tightened her grip on Lena, who gasped for air. "Your friend is now my insurance policy, Dr. Vance."
"Your friend is now my insurance policy, Dr. Vance." Eva's voice was a flat, metallic thing, devoid of the warmth Elara had once mistaken for camaraderie. "You will come with me. You will access that terminal. And you will delete everything. Every file, every log, every trace of this project's true purpose. Then, you will walk away. Forever."

Elara's mind raced, a frantic search for an exit that wasn't there. Lena's eyes met hers, wide with fear, silently begging for a solution that Elara couldn't see. The weight of the pistol against Lena's temple was a physical pressure in the room, a tangible threat that made the air thick and difficult to breathe.

"And if I refuse?" Elara asked, her voice surprisingly steady.

"Then I'll have to make a choice," Eva replied, her thumb caressing the trigger. "I could end this here. Two loose ties cut before they become a problem. Or I could keep your friend as a permanent reminder of the consequences of defiance. The choice is yours, Doctor. But understand that time is not on your side."

Marcus and Aris were still out there, somewhere in the labyrinthine corridors. Were they running? Had they been captured? The uncertainty gnawed at Elara, a constant, sharp tooth in her mind. To surrender now would mean abandoning them, abandoning Dr. Chen, abandoning whatever fragment of her brother might still exist in that tank.

"Let her go," Elara said, her voice barely a whisper. "I'll do what you ask. Just let Lena go."

Eva's lips curled into a thin, humorless smile. "Such nobility. It's almost touching. But no. She stays with me until you've completed your task. Move. To the terminal."

Elara took a slow, deliberate step toward the console, her every muscle coiled with tension. As she reached for the keyboard, her fingers brushed against the cold metal of the emergency wrench she'd dropped earlier. It was a small, insignificant touch, but in that moment, it felt like a lifeline. A desperate, foolish plan began to form in her mind, a Hail Mary thrown against the overwhelming odds. She had to buy time. She had to create an opening. For Lena. For Marcus. For Aris. For everyone trapped in this nightmare of corporate ambition and digital ghosts.

She turned back to face Eva, her expression one of surrender. "Fine," Elara said, her voice hollow. "I'll delete it. But you have to promise me no one else gets hurt. Lena goes free. Now."

Eva's grip on Lena tightened, the pistol pressing harder into her temple. "Your friend is now my insurance policy, Dr. Vance."
