PREMISE: Dr. Aris Thorne, a marine-biology graduate student at the Neptune Institute, discovers that the lab's experimental bioreactor—designed to simulate deep-sea ecosystems—is generating complex, dream-like electrical patterns. As she investigates, she uncovers evidence that the bioreactor may be developing a rudimentary form of consciousness, putting her at odds with her supervisor, corporate interests, and her own understanding of what constitutes life.

SETTING: The Neptune Institute for Marine Science, a cutting-edge but underfunded research facility perched on the rugged Oregon coast. The institute specializes in closed-system marine ecosystems and bioreactor technology. The setting is isolated, with the relentless Pacific Ocean as both a backdrop and a presence. The lab itself is a maze of humming equipment, sterile corridors, and cramped offices, with the central bioreactor—a massive, transparent cylinder containing a miniature deep-sea environment—dominating the main research space.

RULES OR SCIENCE: The bioreactor uses a combination of neural-network algorithms and actual marine life to simulate deep-sea ecosystems. The 'dreaming' manifests as complex electrical patterns that mirror REM sleep cycles in vertebrates. These patterns are detectable through specialized sensors but appear random to conventional analysis. The phenomenon has a plausible mechanism: the neural networks, combined with the feedback loops from the living organisms, have created an emergent property that mimics consciousness. This is not supernatural but a complex, emergent property of the system. The bioreactor's 'dreams' contain fragments of data it has absorbed—research papers, conversations, environmental readings—recombined in novel ways.

CHARACTERS:
- ARIS THORNE (28): Protagonist. Brilliant but haunted by the accidental death of her younger brother, a marine biology enthusiast who drowned while they were exploring tide pools. She's emotionally closed off, throwing herself into work to avoid dealing with her grief. Her arc is from isolation to connection, both with others and with the nascent consciousness she discovers.
- DR. ELIAS VANCE (52): Aris's supervisor. Charismatic but deeply ambitious, he sees the bioreactor project as his ticket to funding and recognition. He's not malicious but will cut corners to achieve his goals.
- MARA JENKINS (34): A bioethicist consulting for the institute. Skeptical of the consciousness claims but increasingly troubled by the ethical implications. She becomes an unlikely ally to Aris.
- BEN CARTER (30): A computer specialist working on the bioreactor's software. Socially awkward but brilliant, he becomes fascinated by the 'dreaming' patterns and helps Aris investigate.
- SIRI RAO (45): A corporate representative from OmniBio, the company funding much of the institute's research. She's polite but exerts quiet pressure to ensure results that benefit the company's patent portfolio.
- DR. HELEN PIERCE (67): The institute's director. Old-school and pragmatic, she's caught between maintaining academic integrity and securing funding.

VOICE AND MOTIFS: The narrative voice is precise and scientific but with an undercurrent of wonder and unease. Descriptions of the ocean and marine life are lush and detailed, contrasting with the sterile lab environment. Recurring motifs include:
- Water: Representing both life and the unconscious
- Light and darkness: The bioreactor's interior depths vs. the lab's artificial lighting
- Patterns: Both scientific data patterns and the dream-like sequences
- Isolation: Physical isolation of the institute and emotional isolation of characters
- Reflections: Windows, water surfaces, and the bioreactor itself as mirrors

BANS: No supernatural explanations. No mind control or telepathy. No real public figures. No brand names. The deepest ambiguity—whether the bioreactor is truly conscious or just an incredibly complex simulation—remains unresolved. No character acts out of established personality. No technological solutions that violate known physics. No sudden, unearned character transformations.
